# Brand and design discovery workflow

Status: Approved supporting workflow  
Last reviewed: 4 August 2026

## Purpose

Gather enough visual direction to produce a coherent, accessible first release without turning the
initial project enquiry into a full design brief. Branding is progressive: the initial form records a
preference, while approved assets and permissions are collected only after a person confirms that the
enquiry is suitable to progress.

## Stage 1 — project enquiry

The eight-step discovery form asks one optional visual-direction question. A short optional note is
revealed only when the customer chooses existing branding, help developing a direction, or another
product to match. Neutral-design and recommendation choices do not reveal another field.

The selection is design evidence, not permission to use an asset and not approved visual scope. The
first release should still have intentional UX, responsive behaviour, clear hierarchy, accessible
interaction, and a themeable component system. Logos, proprietary fonts, photography, illustrations,
and other unverified assets use placeholders at this stage.

## Stage 2 — human review and design pack

If the enquiry is suitable to progress, a Lang Systems reviewer requests only the relevant items:

- approved logo variants and brand guidelines;
- colour values, approved fonts, imagery rules, and existing design tokens;
- two or three words describing the intended tone;
- examples the customer likes or dislikes and what specifically is relevant;
- accessibility, readability, language, device, or environmental requirements; and
- the name or role of the person authorised to approve the visual direction.

Do not ask the customer to resend material already supplied securely. Record who provided each asset,
its source, its allowed purpose, any expiry or attribution requirement, and any known restriction.

## Authority and third-party material

Before using customer-supplied branding in production or a public demonstration, obtain an affirmative
written confirmation tailored to the project. Suggested discussion wording:

> I confirm that I am authorised to provide these brand materials and permit Lang Systems to use them
> for evaluating, designing, developing, testing, and demonstrating this project. I have identified
> any third-party material and the restrictions or attribution requirements that apply.

This wording records project permission; it does not transfer ownership, override a third-party
licence, or replace the final project agreement. The agreed contract must address branding, customer-
supplied material, third-party licences, attribution, portfolio use, confidentiality, and removal or
replacement obligations where relevant. Obtain appropriate legal review before treating template
wording as a final agreement.

## Design and accessibility controls

- Build reusable layout, typography, spacing, colour, focus, error, and component tokens from the
  beginning so branding can change without rebuilding workflows.
- Test customer colours against WCAG 2.2 contrast requirements and never use colour as the only way
  to communicate meaning.
- Obtain human approval of representative screens before applying the theme broadly.
- Keep unapproved or inaccessible brand choices visible as a design issue; do not silently alter or
  approve them.
- Record the final theme, asset versions, approvals, exceptions, and outstanding restrictions in the
  reviewed design and delivery artefacts.

References: [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and
[Understanding use of colour](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color).
